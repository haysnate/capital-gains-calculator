A worked example

Say you are a single filer in Wyoming with $60,000 of taxable income who sells stock held over a year for a $25,000 long-term gain. Federal tax on the gain is $3,750 (all of it lands in the 15% bracket at this income; no NIIT applies below $200,000 MAGI). Wyoming adds nothing, so the total stays $3,750, an effective 15.0% on the gain. These figures use the same math as the calculator, 2026 federal brackets per IRS Rev. Frequently asked questions

Does Wyoming tax capital gains?

No. Wyoming has no personal income tax, so capital gains are not taxed at the state level. Federal capital gains tax still applies.

What will I pay on a long-term gain in Wyoming?

Federally, a long-term gain is taxed at 0%, 15%, or 20% depending on your taxable income, plus the 3.8% NIIT above $200,000 MAGI (single). In Wyoming that federal bill is the whole bill.

Do I still owe federal capital gains tax in Wyoming?

Yes. Federal capital gains tax (0%, 15%, or 20% long-term, ordinary rates short-term, plus the 3.8% NIIT for high earners) applies no matter your state. The free calculator estimates federal, NIIT, and Wyoming tax together.

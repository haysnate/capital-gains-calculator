How South Carolina treats capital gains

South Carolina taxes capital gains as ordinary income on a graduated scale from 1.99% up to a top rate of 5.21%. South Carolina excludes 44% of net long-term capital gains, which the calculator models. The estimate subtracts South Carolina's Income Adjusted Deduction ($15,000 single / $30,000 married), which phases out at higher incomes.

A worked example

Say you are a single filer in South Carolina with $60,000 of taxable income who sells stock held over a year for a $25,000 long-term gain. Federal tax on the gain is $3,750 (all of it lands in the 15% bracket at this income; no NIIT applies below $200,000 MAGI). South Carolina adds an estimated $1,085, for a combined $4,835, an effective 19.3% on the gain. These figures use the same math as the calculator, 2026 federal brackets per IRS Rev. Does South Carolina tax capital gains?

What will I pay on a long-term gain in South Carolina?

Combined, our worked example ($25,000 long-term gain on $60,000 of income, single) comes to $4,835: $3,750 federal plus $1,085 South Carolina tax, an effective 19.3%.

Do I still owe federal capital gains tax in South Carolina?

Yes. Federal capital gains tax (0%, 15%, or 20% long-term, ordinary rates short-term, plus the 3.8% NIIT for high earners) applies no matter your state. The free calculator estimates federal, NIIT, and South Carolina tax together.

Connecticut Capital Gains Tax (2026)

By Nathan Hays · Updated July 26, 2026

Connecticut taxes capital gains as ordinary income on a graduated scale from 2% up to a top rate of 6.99%. Run your own numbers with the free capital gains tax calculator, which estimates federal, NIIT, and Connecticut tax together.

In short: federal long-term rates of 0%, 15%, or 20% apply everywhere. Connecticut adds state tax on top, at up to 6.99%.

How Connecticut treats capital gains

Connecticut taxes capital gains as ordinary income on a graduated scale from 2% up to a top rate of 6.99%.

A worked example

Say you are a single filer in Connecticut with $60,000 of taxable income who sells stock held over a year for a $25,000 long-term gain. Federal tax on the gain is $3,750 (all of it lands in the 15% bracket at this income; no NIIT applies below $200,000 MAGI). Connecticut adds an estimated $1,375, for a combined $5,125, an effective 20.5% on the gain. These figures use the same math as the calculator, 2026 federal brackets per IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32, and state figures from the Tax Foundation's State Individual Income Tax Rates and Brackets, 2026.

Frequently asked questions

Does Connecticut tax capital gains?

Yes. Connecticut taxes capital gains as ordinary income. Connecticut taxes capital gains as ordinary income on a graduated scale from 2% up to a top rate of 6.99%.

What will I pay on a long-term gain in Connecticut?

Combined, our worked example ($25,000 long-term gain on $60,000 of income, single) comes to $5,125: $3,750 federal plus $1,375 Connecticut tax, an effective 20.5%.

Do I still owe federal capital gains tax in Connecticut?

Yes. Federal capital gains tax (0%, 15%, or 20% long-term, ordinary rates short-term, plus the 3.8% NIIT for high earners) applies no matter your state. The free calculator estimates federal, NIIT, and Connecticut tax together.

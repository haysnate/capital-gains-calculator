How North Dakota treats capital gains

North Dakota taxes capital gains as ordinary income on a graduated scale from 0% up to a top rate of 2.5%. North Dakota excludes 40% of net long-term capital gains, which the calculator models. North Dakota uses the federal standard deduction ($16,100 single / $32,200 married in 2026), which the estimate subtracts first.

Good news if you hold long. North Dakota excludes 40% of net long-term capital gains from state tax, and the calculator models that exclusion.

A worked example

Say you are a single filer in North Dakota with $60,000 of taxable income who sells stock held over a year for a $25,000 long-term gain. Federal tax on the gain is $3,750 (all of it lands in the 15% bracket at this income; no NIIT applies below $200,000 MAGI). North Dakota adds an estimated $203, for a combined $3,953, an effective 15.8% on the gain. These figures use the same math as the calculator, 2026 federal brackets per IRS Rev. What will I pay on a long-term gain in North Dakota?

Combined, our worked example ($25,000 long-term gain on $60,000 of income, single) comes to $3,953: $3,750 federal plus $203 North Dakota tax, an effective 15.8%.

Do I still owe federal capital gains tax in North Dakota?

Yes. Federal capital gains tax (0%, 15%, or 20% long-term, ordinary rates short-term, plus the 3.8% NIIT for high earners) applies no matter your state. The free calculator estimates federal, NIIT, and North Dakota tax together.

West Virginia Capital Gains Tax (2026)

By Nathan Hays · Updated August 17, 2026

West Virginia taxes capital gains as ordinary income on a graduated scale from 2.11% up to a top rate of 4.58%. Run your own numbers with the free capital gains tax calculator, which estimates federal, NIIT, and West Virginia tax together.

In short: federal long-term rates of 0%, 15%, or 20% apply everywhere. West Virginia adds state tax on top, at up to 4.58%.

How West Virginia treats capital gains

West Virginia taxes capital gains as ordinary income on a graduated scale from 2.11% up to a top rate of 4.58%.

Parts of West Virginia also levy local income taxes that are not included in these estimates.

A worked example

Say you are a single filer in West Virginia with $60,000 of taxable income who sells stock held over a year for a $25,000 long-term gain. Federal tax on the gain is $3,750 (all of it lands in the 15% bracket at this income; no NIIT applies below $200,000 MAGI). West Virginia adds an estimated $1,145, for a combined $4,895, an effective 19.6% on the gain. These figures use the same math as the calculator, 2026 federal brackets per IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32, and state figures from the Tax Foundation's State Individual Income Tax Rates and Brackets, 2026.

Frequently asked questions

Does West Virginia tax capital gains?

Yes. West Virginia taxes capital gains as ordinary income. West Virginia taxes capital gains as ordinary income on a graduated scale from 2.11% up to a top rate of 4.58%.

What will I pay on a long-term gain in West Virginia?

Combined, our worked example ($25,000 long-term gain on $60,000 of income, single) comes to $4,895: $3,750 federal plus $1,145 West Virginia tax, an effective 19.6%.

Do I still owe federal capital gains tax in West Virginia?

Yes. Federal capital gains tax (0%, 15%, or 20% long-term, ordinary rates short-term, plus the 3.8% NIIT for high earners) applies no matter your state. The free calculator estimates federal, NIIT, and West Virginia tax together.

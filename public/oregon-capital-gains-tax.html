Oregon Capital Gains Tax (2026)

By Nathan Hays · Updated August 17, 2026

Oregon taxes capital gains as ordinary income on a graduated scale from 4.75% up to a top rate of 9.9%. Run your own numbers with the free capital gains tax calculator, which estimates federal, NIIT, and Oregon tax together.

In short: federal long-term rates of 0%, 15%, or 20% apply everywhere. Oregon adds state tax on top, at up to 9.9%.

How Oregon treats capital gains

Oregon taxes capital gains as ordinary income on a graduated scale from 4.75% up to a top rate of 9.9%. The estimate subtracts Oregon's standard deduction ($2,910 single / $5,820 married).

Parts of Oregon also levy local income taxes that are not included in these estimates.

A worked example

Say you are a single filer in Oregon with $60,000 of taxable income who sells stock held over a year for a $25,000 long-term gain. Federal tax on the gain is $3,750 (all of it lands in the 15% bracket at this income; no NIIT applies below $200,000 MAGI). Oregon adds an estimated $2,188, for a combined $5,938, an effective 23.8% on the gain. These figures use the same math as the calculator, 2026 federal brackets per IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32, and state figures from the Tax Foundation's State Individual Income Tax Rates and Brackets, 2026.

Frequently asked questions

Does Oregon tax capital gains?

Yes. Oregon taxes capital gains as ordinary income. Oregon taxes capital gains as ordinary income on a graduated scale from 4.75% up to a top rate of 9.9%.

What will I pay on a long-term gain in Oregon?

Combined, our worked example ($25,000 long-term gain on $60,000 of income, single) comes to $5,938: $3,750 federal plus $2,188 Oregon tax, an effective 23.8%.

Do I still owe federal capital gains tax in Oregon?

Yes. Federal capital gains tax (0%, 15%, or 20% long-term, ordinary rates short-term, plus the 3.8% NIIT for high earners) applies no matter your state. The free calculator estimates federal, NIIT, and Oregon tax together.

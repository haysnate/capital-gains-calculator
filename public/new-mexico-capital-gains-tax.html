New Mexico Capital Gains Tax (2026)

By Nathan Hays · Updated August 17, 2026

New Mexico taxes capital gains as ordinary income on a graduated scale from 1.5% up to a top rate of 5.9%. Run your own numbers with the free capital gains tax calculator, which estimates federal, NIIT, and New Mexico tax together.

In short: federal long-term rates of 0%, 15%, or 20% apply everywhere. New Mexico adds state tax on top, at up to 5.9%.

How New Mexico treats capital gains

New Mexico taxes capital gains as ordinary income on a graduated scale from 1.5% up to a top rate of 5.9%. New Mexico uses the federal standard deduction ($16,100 single / $32,200 married in 2026), which the estimate subtracts first.

A modest break on long-term gains. Since 2025 New Mexico allows a deduction of the greater of up to $2,500 of net capital gains (long-term gains on any asset, modeled by the calculator) or 40% of up to $1 million of gain from selling a New Mexico business (not modeled). Short-term gains get no deduction.

A worked example

Say you are a single filer in New Mexico with $60,000 of taxable income who sells stock held over a year for a $25,000 long-term gain. Federal tax on the gain is $3,750 (all of it lands in the 15% bracket at this income; no NIIT applies below $200,000 MAGI). New Mexico adds an estimated $1,058, for a combined $4,808, an effective 19.2% on the gain. These figures use the same math as the calculator, 2026 federal brackets per IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32, and state figures from the Tax Foundation's State Individual Income Tax Rates and Brackets, 2026.

Frequently asked questions

Does New Mexico tax capital gains?

Yes. New Mexico taxes capital gains as ordinary income. New Mexico taxes capital gains as ordinary income on a graduated scale from 1.5% up to a top rate of 5.9%.

What will I pay on a long-term gain in New Mexico?

Combined, our worked example ($25,000 long-term gain on $60,000 of income, single) comes to $4,808: $3,750 federal plus $1,058 New Mexico tax, an effective 19.2%.

Do I still owe federal capital gains tax in New Mexico?

Yes. Federal capital gains tax (0%, 15%, or 20% long-term, ordinary rates short-term, plus the 3.8% NIIT for high earners) applies no matter your state. The free calculator estimates federal, NIIT, and New Mexico tax together.

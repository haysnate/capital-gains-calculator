Rhode Island Capital Gains Tax (2026)

By Nathan Hays · Updated August 17, 2026

Rhode Island taxes capital gains as ordinary income on a graduated scale from 3.75% up to a top rate of 5.99%. Run your own numbers with the free capital gains tax calculator, which estimates federal, NIIT, and Rhode Island tax together.

In short: federal long-term rates of 0%, 15%, or 20% apply everywhere. Rhode Island adds state tax on top, at up to 5.99%.

How Rhode Island treats capital gains

Rhode Island taxes capital gains as ordinary income on a graduated scale from 3.75% up to a top rate of 5.99%. The estimate subtracts Rhode Island's standard deduction ($11,200 single / $22,400 married).

A worked example

Say you are a single filer in Rhode Island with $60,000 of taxable income who sells stock held over a year for a $25,000 long-term gain. Federal tax on the gain is $3,750 (all of it lands in the 15% bracket at this income; no NIIT applies below $200,000 MAGI). Rhode Island adds an estimated $938, for a combined $4,688, an effective 18.8% on the gain. These figures use the same math as the calculator, 2026 federal brackets per IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32, and state figures from the Tax Foundation's State Individual Income Tax Rates and Brackets, 2026.

Frequently asked questions

Does Rhode Island tax capital gains?

Yes. Rhode Island taxes capital gains as ordinary income. Rhode Island taxes capital gains as ordinary income on a graduated scale from 3.75% up to a top rate of 5.99%.

What will I pay on a long-term gain in Rhode Island?

Combined, our worked example ($25,000 long-term gain on $60,000 of income, single) comes to $4,688: $3,750 federal plus $938 Rhode Island tax, an effective 18.8%.

Do I still owe federal capital gains tax in Rhode Island?

Yes. Federal capital gains tax (0%, 15%, or 20% long-term, ordinary rates short-term, plus the 3.8% NIIT for high earners) applies no matter your state. The free calculator estimates federal, NIIT, and Rhode Island tax together.

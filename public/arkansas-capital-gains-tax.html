In short: federal long-term rates of 0%, 15%, or 20% apply everywhere. Arkansas adds state tax on top, at up to 3.9%.

How Arkansas treats capital gains

Arkansas taxes capital gains as ordinary income on a graduated scale from 2% up to a top rate of 3.9%. Arkansas excludes 50% of net long-term capital gains, which the calculator models. The estimate subtracts Arkansas's standard deduction ($2,470 single / $4,940 married).

Good news if you hold long. Arkansas excludes 50% of net long-term capital gains from state tax, and the calculator models that exclusion.

A worked example

Say you are a single filer in Arkansas with $60,000 of taxable income who sells stock held over a year for a $25,000 long-term gain. Federal tax on the gain is $3,750 (all of it lands in the 15% bracket at this income; no NIIT applies below $200,000 MAGI). Arkansas adds an estimated $488, for a combined $4,238, an effective 17.0% on the gain. These figures use the same math as the calculator, 2026 federal brackets per IRS Rev. Frequently asked questions

Does Arkansas tax capital gains?

Yes. Arkansas taxes capital gains as ordinary income. Arkansas taxes capital gains as ordinary income on a graduated scale from 2% up to a top rate of 3.9%. Arkansas excludes 50% of net long-term capital gains, which the calculator models.

What will I pay on a long-term gain in Arkansas?

Combined, our worked example ($25,000 long-term gain on $60,000 of income, single) comes to $4,238: $3,750 federal plus $488 Arkansas tax, an effective 17.0%.

Do I still owe federal capital gains tax in Arkansas?

Yes. Federal capital gains tax (0%, 15%, or 20% long-term, ordinary rates short-term, plus the 3.8% NIIT for high earners) applies no matter your state. The free calculator estimates federal, NIIT, and Arkansas tax together.

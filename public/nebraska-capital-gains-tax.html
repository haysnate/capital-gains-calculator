Nebraska Capital Gains Tax (2026)

By Nathan Hays · Updated July 26, 2026

Nebraska taxes capital gains as ordinary income on a graduated scale from 2.46% up to a top rate of 4.55%. Run your own numbers with the free capital gains tax calculator, which estimates federal, NIIT, and Nebraska tax together.

In short: federal long-term rates of 0%, 15%, or 20% apply everywhere. Nebraska adds state tax on top, at up to 4.55%.

How Nebraska treats capital gains

Nebraska taxes capital gains as ordinary income on a graduated scale from 2.46% up to a top rate of 4.55%. The estimate subtracts Nebraska's standard deduction ($8,850 single / $17,700 married).

A worked example

Say you are a single filer in Nebraska with $60,000 of taxable income who sells stock held over a year for a $25,000 long-term gain. Federal tax on the gain is $3,750 (all of it lands in the 15% bracket at this income; no NIIT applies below $200,000 MAGI). Nebraska adds an estimated $1,138, for a combined $4,888, an effective 19.6% on the gain. These figures use the same math as the calculator, 2026 federal brackets per IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32, and state figures from the Tax Foundation's State Individual Income Tax Rates and Brackets, 2026.

Frequently asked questions

Does Nebraska tax capital gains?

Yes. Nebraska taxes capital gains as ordinary income. Nebraska taxes capital gains as ordinary income on a graduated scale from 2.46% up to a top rate of 4.55%.

What will I pay on a long-term gain in Nebraska?

Combined, our worked example ($25,000 long-term gain on $60,000 of income, single) comes to $4,888: $3,750 federal plus $1,138 Nebraska tax, an effective 19.6%.

Do I still owe federal capital gains tax in Nebraska?

Yes. Federal capital gains tax (0%, 15%, or 20% long-term, ordinary rates short-term, plus the 3.8% NIIT for high earners) applies no matter your state. The free calculator estimates federal, NIIT, and Nebraska tax together.
